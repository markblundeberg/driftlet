A redox couple at an electrode: cyclic voltammetry

Fe³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺ at platinum, in 0.5 M KCl, through a 20 µm stagnant layer to a stirred bulk. Slow scans give the steady, sigmoidal wave of a convective electrode; fast ones outrun diffusion and give the peaks of a still solution. A small rate constant pulls the waves apart.

The first diagram is electronic: the platinum's Fermi level, and the couple's redox level, the electronic level at which the local Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ mixture is in equilibrium. The gap between them at the surface is the overpotential, which drives Butler–Volmer kinetics; the redox level's slope across the layer is concentration polarisation. It sits VT ln(c₃/c₂) from the couple's standard level, which in turn rides a fixed distance above the SHE's standard level, φ itself with table μ°. That one is nearly level thanks to the supporting electrolyte, and "vs SHE" means against it in the stirred bulk, at the right. The last diagram is the same story ion by ion: each species' voltage Vi and standard level V°i (K⁺, about 3 V lower, is left out).
