A redox couple at an electrode: cyclic voltammetry

Cyclic voltammetry of Fe³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺ at platinum, in 0.5 M KCl: the voltammogram as it's swept, and the electrode's Fermi level, the couple's redox level and each ion's level across the layer of solution by the electrode. Vary the scan rate, the stirring (as the thickness of a stagnant layer) and the rate constant k⁰. (More about the model below.)

About this simulation

The electrode faces a stagnant layer, then a stirred bulk. Only the solution within a few √(Dt) of the electrode is ever converted; the bulk is a fixed reservoir. A thin layer stands for vigorous stirring (a rotating disk): once the depleted zone fills it, the wave is steady and sigmoidal. A thick one is a still solution, where the depleted zone keeps growing through the sweep and the current peaks then sags: the classic "duck". The rate constant k⁰ is per area at the reference concentration (1 M): in the cm/s of tables, k° = 0.1·k⁰. The peaks' separation depends on it and the scan rate only together, through Nicholson's ψ ∝ k°/√(Dv) (Anal. Chem. 37, 1351, 1965). Reversible, they're 57.5 mV apart (near 2.22 RT/F, the limit for switching potentials far past the wave; the 59 mV often quoted is 2.3 RT/F, a rule of thumb); without the ohmic drop, at 0.1 V/s, k⁰ = 10 gives 57.8 mV, k⁰ = 1 gives 59.9, and k⁰ = 0.1 (ψ ≈ 1) gives 80, and each decade faster a scan needs √10 times the k⁰ for the same. The layer's ohmic drop adds to it: 65 mV apart through 1 mm at 0.1 V/s with k⁰ = 10. The peak currents grow as √v, whatever the kinetics hold the wave to.

The dashed line is the same experiment worked out apart from the solver, for the cycle under way: each iron ion diffusing through the stagnant layer (a finite-volume scheme of its own, with implicit steps), Butler–Volmer at the platinum in the same form, and the layer's ohmic drop. A thick layer settles slowly, cycle after cycle (its diffusion time L²/D is half an hour at 1 mm), so the comparison is cycle by cycle. The simulated cycle follows it within about 1% of the peak current for every setting of the controls. What the dashed line leaves out is migration: the iron ions carry a little of the current through the field in the layer, and with 5 mM of them against 0.5 M of KCl that's most of the 1% (with a tenth of the iron, 0.1% at slow scans; at fast ones the simulation's own resolution, up to 0.3%, is what's left; a separate Nernst–Planck calculation with migration in it, run once for this page, closed the gap to 0.005% where the simulation's grid was fine). The readout's peaks are the last whole cycle's. A wave whose current is largest at a switching potential has none there: it has levelled off (a thin layer, the supply steady) or is still rising (kinetics too slow to reach it). (The textbook's Randles–Ševčík peak current is for a first sweep through a solution of one form of the couple; here each cycle starts from what the last left at the surface, and the peaks are 1.7 to 1.8 times its value for 5 mM.)

What it leaves out: an ideal solution, without the acid real Fe³⁺ needs to stay in solution (in 1 M HCl its formal potential is nearer 0.70 V than the tables' 0.77); no double layer, so no charging current (every real voltammogram's baseline: about 2% of the peak at 10 V/s); the platinum's own oxide at the top of the window; and a reference electrode at the stagnant layer's far edge, so the ohmic drop is the layer's alone.

The first level diagram is electronic: the platinum's Fermi level, and the couple's redox level, the electronic level at which the local Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ mixture is in equilibrium. The gap between them at the surface is the overpotential, which drives Butler–Volmer kinetics; the redox level's slope across the layer is concentration polarisation. It sits VT ln(c₃/c₂) from the couple's standard level, which in turn rides a fixed distance above the SHE's standard level, φ itself with table μ°. That one is nearly level thanks to the supporting electrolyte, and "vs SHE" means against it in the stirred bulk, at the right.

The last diagram is the same story ion by ion, using the species voltage Vi = μ̄i/(ziF): an ion's electrochemical potential per unit charge, in volts, so it can be drawn on the same axis as the electrons' Fermi level. Each ion moves down its own μ̄i, so its current flows down slopes in its own Vi (thick; cations move downhill, anions uphill); the thin line under each, the standard level V°i, is where Vi would sit at the reference concentration. K⁺, Cl⁻ and Fe²⁺ are drawn with display offsets (marked ⌇), so that all share one readable axis.
