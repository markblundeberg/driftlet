A redox couple at an electrode: cyclic voltammetry

Cyclic voltammetry of Fe³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺ at platinum, in 0.5 M KCl: the voltammogram as it's swept, and the electrode's Fermi level, the couple's redox level and each ion's level across the layer of solution by the electrode. Vary the scan rate, the stirring (as the thickness of a stagnant layer) and the rate constant k⁰. (More about the model below.)

About this simulation

The electrode faces a stagnant layer, then a stirred bulk. Only the solution within a few √(Dt) of the electrode is ever converted; the bulk is a fixed reservoir. A thin layer stands for vigorous stirring (a rotating disk): once the depleted zone fills it, the wave is steady and sigmoidal. A thick one is a still solution, where the depleted zone keeps growing through the sweep and the current peaks then sags: the classic "duck". With fast kinetics (k⁰ ≳ 1) its peaks sit 57 mV apart (2.22 RT/F, a reversible couple's with these switching potentials; the 59 mV often quoted is 2.3 RT/F, a rule of thumb), plus the ohmic drop through the layer: 64 mV apart through 1 mm at 0.1 V/s. A smaller rate constant pulls them further apart (quasi-reversible, then irreversible). Faster scans outrun diffusion.

The dashed line is the same experiment worked out apart from the solver, once it has settled into a repeating cycle: each iron ion diffusing through the stagnant layer (a finite-volume scheme of its own, with implicit steps), Butler–Volmer at the platinum in the same form, and the layer's ohmic drop. The simulated cycle follows it within 1% of the peak current for every setting of the controls. What the dashed line leaves out is migration: the iron ions carry a little of the current through the field in the layer, and with 5 mM of them against 0.5 M of KCl that's the 1% (with a tenth of the iron, it's 0.1%). The peaks in the readout are the last whole cycle's; a wave that only levels off, its current largest at the sweep's ends, has none. (The textbook's Randles–Ševčík peak current is for a first sweep through a solution of one form of the couple; here each cycle starts from what the last left at the surface.)

The first level diagram is electronic: the platinum's Fermi level, and the couple's redox level, the electronic level at which the local Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ mixture is in equilibrium. The gap between them at the surface is the overpotential, which drives Butler–Volmer kinetics; the redox level's slope across the layer is concentration polarisation. It sits VT ln(c₃/c₂) from the couple's standard level, which in turn rides a fixed distance above the SHE's standard level, φ itself with table μ°. That one is nearly level thanks to the supporting electrolyte, and "vs SHE" means against it in the stirred bulk, at the right.

The last diagram is the same story ion by ion, using the species voltage Vi = μ̄i/(ziF): an ion's electrochemical potential per unit charge, in volts, so it can be drawn on the same axis as the electrons' Fermi level. Each ion moves down its own μ̄i, so its current flows down slopes in its own Vi (thick; cations move downhill, anions uphill); the thin line under each, the standard level V°i, is where Vi would sit at the reference concentration. K⁺, about 3 V lower, is left out.
