A rotating disk electrode: Levich and Koutecký–Levich

A platinum disk spinning in 1 mM Fe³⁺ and Fe²⁺ (in 1 M KCl): the steady current against potential at five rotation rates, and the analysis an electrochemist would run on them, Koutecký–Levich plots to take the transport out and a Tafel plot of what's left, which gives back the electrode's rate constant k⁰ and transfer coefficient α. Vary k⁰ and the potential the Koutecký–Levich plot is read at. (More about the model below.)

About this simulation

A spinning disk pulls solution up to its face and flings it outward, and the flow thins the layer the ions must diffuse across to the same thickness everywhere on the disk, Levich's δ = 1.61 D1/3ν1/6ω−1/2. So the limiting current grows as √ω (Levich's equation), and at any potential the current is the kinetics and the transport in series: 1/i = 1/ik + 1/(Bω1/2), a straight line against ω−1/2 whose intercept is the current the electrode would pass with no transport limit at all. Those intercepts, one per potential, make the Tafel plot: corrected for the back reaction (ik/(1 − eFη/RT) with equal Fe³⁺ and Fe²⁺), its slope is αF/RT and it meets η = 0 at Fk⁰c.

The page does that analysis on the simulated currents alone, as on measured ones: a straight-line fit per potential across the five rotation rates, and a line through the corrected intercepts where they're well determined (where the kinetic current is at most ten times the fastest disk's limiting current; past that the intercept is the small difference of big numbers, as with real data). The readout gives what comes back against what went in. With k⁰ large the wave is all transport, the intercepts sink into that difference, and few points are left to fit: an electrode too fast for a disk to measure.

The model stands one stagnant layer of Levich's thickness for Fe³⁺ in for the flow: the flux to the disk is then exactly Levich's (the disk is uniformly accessible, so a layer of that thickness carries the same flux), though the profile across it is a straight line where the real one curves. Fe²⁺'s layer would be 6% thicker (δ goes as D1/3); here they share one. The lines are the exact steady solution of this model, i = F(kfc − kbc)/(1 + kfδ/DO + kbδ/DR), less migration, which the 1 M KCl keeps to 0.1% (it scales with the iron's concentration, and the analysis puts it into the slopes: k⁰ comes back within 0.02%). ν = 1 mm²/s, water's.
