A redox electrode's impedance: the Randles circuit

Platinum in 1 mM Fe³⁺ and Fe²⁺ with KCl, at rest, behind a stagnant layer of solution: its impedance spectrum, simulated from the ions and the reaction, against the Randles equivalent circuit with every element worked out from the same physics. A semicircle for the reaction and its double layer, then diffusion's arc: a 45° line while the diffusion is short of the stirred bulk (a thick stagnant layer shows it best), bending back once it reaches it. Vary the rate constant, the salt and the stagnant layer. (More about the model below.)

About this simulation

A small sine wave of voltage on the electrode drives three things at once. Charge piles onto the double layer, like a capacitor Cdl. Fe³⁺ is reduced and Fe²⁺ oxidised a little faster and slower in turn, which near rest is a resistor, the charge-transfer resistance Rct = RT/(Fi0), with i0 = Fk⁰c the exchange current. And the reaction's own small changes in concentration have to diffuse to and from the bulk, which lags: the Warburg impedance ZW. The current to all of them crosses the solution, Rs. So Z = Rs + 1/(iωCdl + 1/(Rct + ZW)), Randles' circuit. The semicircle's top sits at ω = 1/(RctCdl); below that frequency the Warburg's 45° line takes over; and once the diffusion reaches through the stagnant layer to the stirred bulk (about D/L²), ZW stops growing and the spectrum bends back to the real axis at Rs + Rct + RW, with RW = (RTL/F²c)(1/DFe³⁺ + 1/DFe²⁺).

driftlet knows nothing of the circuit: it solves the ions, the potential and the reaction for their small oscillation at each frequency. The circuit's elements here come from the same physics, by hand: Rs = L/κ from the solution's conductivity; Cdl, an inner (Stern) layer of 20 µF/cm² in series with the diffuse layer's εκD (Gouy–Chapman at zero charge, which the electrode is held at); Rct from k⁰; and ZW for a layer of thickness L ending at a fixed bulk, (RT/F²c)·Σ tanh(L√(iω/D))/√(iωD) over Fe³⁺ and Fe²⁺. The readout gives how far the two disagree, under 1% across these controls. Most of that shrinks with less iron: the iron's own migration, and its share in charging the double layer, which the circuit leaves out. The rest, a few tenths of a percent in the most dilute salt, isn't traced here. (Not the Frumkin effect: at rest a change in ψ moves the forward and back reactions alike, zFe³⁺ − zFe²⁺ = 1 being the electron's own charge, so Rct sees the whole of the overpotential.)

The double layer is below the grid (driftlet's diffuse: { epsr }), set so the electrode has no charge at rest: off its zero charge, ψ would shift the reaction's rate (the Frumkin effect, on its own page) and the diffuse layer's capacitance, and the circuit's elements would need those corrections too. k⁰ is illustrative.
