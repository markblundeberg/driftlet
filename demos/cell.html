Ag | AgNO₃ | Ag: concentration polarisation, live

10 mM silver nitrate between two silver electrodes 20 µm apart, as a strictly neutral electrolyte, driven by a square wave of cell voltage. Ag⁺ is plated at one electrode and stripped at the other; NO₃⁻ can't cross either. So each reversal starts with a burst of current limited only by the solution's resistance; then salt piles up at one electrode and depletes at the other, and the current sags toward its steady value, (4FD₊c/L) tanh(FV/4RT) (dashed), which can never exceed the limiting current 4FD₊c/L. Half-periods shorter than the diffusion time, (L/2)²/D ≈ 60 ms here, reverse the salt before it has piled up, and the current stays near ohmic.

The diagram is ion by ion: each species' voltage Vi = μ̄i/(ziF) (thick) and standard level V°i (thin). Ag⁺'s voltage slopes from one electrode's to the other's, driving the current. NO₃⁻'s is level once it settles, since nitrate carries no current; meanwhile its standard level is sloped, and that slope is the salt's concentration gradient. The impedance spectrum at rest is the same physics in frequency: the bulk resistance, then a finite-length Warburg arc from salt diffusion (theory dashed).
