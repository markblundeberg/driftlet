MOS capacitor: accumulation, depletion, inversion

p-type silicon under 5 nm of oxide and a metal gate. The gate voltage sets the gate metal's Fermi level; the silicon's bands bend at the surface to meet it, and φ (dotted) drops straight across the oxide, its slope the oxide field. (In the silicon φ runs along the conduction band edge, where the library's data anchor it.) Negative bias pulls holes to the surface (accumulation), moderate positive bias pushes them away (depletion), and beyond threshold electrons gather in a thin inversion layer. Those electrons come from a port holding them at ground beside the oxide, the 1D stand-in for a transistor's source and drain; without it, they'd have to diffuse in from the back contact.

The capacitance at 1 kHz follows the ideal low-frequency C–V curve (dashed: the oxide in series with the silicon's dQ/dψ, from the closed-form surface charge), to four digits. Flat band is at VFB = zeroCharge + φbulk, set by the gate interface's work-function difference; threshold is where the surface has inverted as strongly as the bulk is doped, ψs = 2φF. More doping widens the window between them and deepens the dip.
