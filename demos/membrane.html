A cation-exchange membrane between two salt solutions

A membrane with fixed negative charge separates 0.1 M NaCl from a more dilute solution, at open circuit, with a Ag/AgCl electrode in each solution. The fixed charge keeps chloride out (Donnan exclusion), so the salt that leaks through is carried almost entirely by Na⁺. The diagram shows why the cell has a voltage: each ion's species voltage Vi = μ̄i/(ziF). Na⁺ stays nearly level across the membrane, in equilibrium between the solutions, so the whole salt difference lands on Cl⁻, which the electrodes sense: E = 2t₊VT ln(c₁/c₂), with t₊ → 1 for an ideal membrane. (The often-quoted 59 mV per decade "membrane potential" is this minus the electrodes' own Nernst terms, a conventional split.) With less fixed charge, chloride gets in and t₊ falls to its free-solution value, D₊/(D₊ + D₋) = 0.40.
